# Thesis references in the code

For each entry [1]–[28] of the thesis bibliography: its key in `docs/references.bib`, where the
thesis cites it, and where `Feec/` cites it (module docstrings, and the declarations whose
docstrings carry the citation). Citations follow the convention of `docs/REFACTOR_PLAN.md`
(`[Key], loc`, with a `## References` entry in every citing module) and are checked by
`scripts/CheckCitations.sh`. Thesis locations use the numbering of
`[QuennevilleBelair2015]`; page numbers inside the references are the thesis' own and were not
checked against the sources.

All 28 references are cited in the code. Some individual thesis sites concern material that is
not formalized; they are listed under "Sites without a Lean counterpart" below the table.

## Sites without a Lean counterpart

These thesis sites cite a reference for material that is not formalized; the reference itself is
cited elsewhere (table above), so no reference is left uncited.

- [6], §2.1: abstract-index notation convention. Pure notation; the code works with coordinate
  index functions.
- [9] p. 42, §2.2: Sylvester's law of inertia (signature is basis-independent). Not formalized;
  `Feec.Algebra.Metric` fixes the Minkowski signature explicitly.
- [9] p. 33, §2.3: tensoriality of `∇[c∇d]vᵃ`. Not formalized; the Riemann tensor is defined by
  its coordinate formula (2.2) (`Feec.CurvatureTensor.riemComp`, `riemField`).
- [6], §2.5: diffeomorphisms. Not formalized; gauge freedom is modelled infinitesimally by a gauge
  vector (`Feec.LinearizedGravity.gaugeTR`, `Hfun_gauge`); see the scope note of
  `Feec.Physics.LinearizedGravity.Gauge`.
- [9] p. 20 and [6] p. 439, §2.6: Lie derivative via flows. Not formalized; only its outcome
  `2∂₍ₐξ_b₎` enters as a definition (`gaugeTR`, `Hjet_gauge`, `Hfun_gauge`).
- [2], nonlinear vacuum EB system (Ch. 3 intro, §3.4), and [3], nonlinear analogue of Prop 3.2:
  only the linearized system is formalized; the docstrings of `Feec.EinsteinBianchi.linBianchi_iff`
  and `EBJet` cite them as the nonlinear analogue.
- [13], [14], [7]: motivation or generic background; cited at module level only.
